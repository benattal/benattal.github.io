We train a neural radiance cache at 3D surface points to accelerate rendering. Supervision comes from two sources — eye paths starting at the camera and light paths starting at the light source — constructed to be disjoint, so their sum covers all transport without double-counting.

Why the density factor matters

It may be tempting to skip the factor and train the cache directly on the per-path average . A focusing lens makes the problem concrete. A collimated light source illuminates the entire scene — rays that miss the lens land directly on the floor, while rays that pass through the lens converge to a focal point, concentrating many paths into a tiny region.

The collimated light illuminates the entire floor: rays that miss the lens land directly (teal, uniform), while rays through the lens converge to the focal point (coral, concentrated). Every ray carries identical throughput — the per-path average is flat (dashed teal line), matching the uniform background. But the true radiance has a sharp spike at the focus (solid coral curve) where the lens concentrates many rays into a tiny area. The green-shaded spike is the caustic contribution that uncorrected supervision misses — the focal spot is actually 8× brighter than the background.

! The flat line matches the direct-illumination background but misses the focused spike. Uncorrected: (flat). Correct: (base + 8× spike at focus).

The lens preserves per-ray throughput while concentrating path density — only captures this focusing. Either we estimate it, or the cache is blind to caustics.

Color supervision convergence

Why the light color supervision converges correctly

The light color NGP and the zero-supervision loss share a single network. L2 regression converges to the frequency-weighted expected target at each point — and the scale factor in the light loss is designed to cancel with the visit-frequency denominator.

1

The visit frequency at is proportional to each path type's area density. The zero term drops out.

2

The scale factor in the light loss target cancels with the visit-frequency denominator. This is the reason for that scale factor — without it, the zero-supervision term would introduce a systematic bias.
